Fevicol is so dominant that many carpenters use the name for any white wood glue. But India has several capable wood adhesive brands — national, regional and local — and switching can improve margins for dealers and cost per job for contractors. The key is to compare **fairly**: grade-for-grade, on real jobs.

## Step 1: Compare like-for-like grades

Wood adhesives are sold in durability classes. Never compare a D1 economy glue from one brand with a D3 premium glue from another. Map each product to its class first:

| Use | Class to compare |
|---|---|
| Economy dry-interior carpentry | D1 |
| Professional furniture, laminates, veneer | D2 |
| Kitchens, bathrooms, coastal homes | D3 |

Background: [D1–D4 wood adhesive classes explained](/learning-center/d1-d2-d3-d4-wood-adhesive-classes-en-204-explained).

## Step 2: Run the same tests side by side

1. **Spread test** — same spreader, same board. Which spreads more smoothly and evenly?
2. **Open time** — how long before each skins on your bench, in your weather?
3. **Dry bond test** — glue offcuts, clamp equally, cure fully, break. Look for **wood tear**.
4. **Water-soak test** (for D2/D3) — soak cured joints, then break. D3 should still hold strongly.
5. **Laminate test** — paste a real laminate on a real board; check for bubbles and edge lift after a week.

## Step 3: Calculate cost per sheet, not price per kg

Record how many grams each adhesive needs for a standard 8×4 sheet. Then:

**Cost per sheet = price per kg × kg per sheet**

A slightly cheaper adhesive that needs more per sheet can cost more. Use the [coverage calculator](/tools/coverage-calculator) as a starting point, then measure real use.

## Step 4: Look beyond the tub

- **Availability** — can your nearest dealer keep all grades in stock?
- **Pack sizes** — small packs for site work, drums for workshops.
- **Consistency** — does it behave the same every drum?
- **Support** — who helps if a job fails?
- **Dealer margins** — for shop owners, margin and replenishment speed matter as much as product.

## Step 5: Switch on one job, then scale

Use the new adhesive on one kitchen or one set of wardrobes, monitor it through a few weeks (and ideally a monsoon), then roll out.
