Whether you're launching a regional paint brand or optimising an existing range, every **water-based emulsion paint** is built from the same families of raw materials. Understanding what each one does is the fastest way to improve performance and cut cost. This primer walks through a typical interior/exterior emulsion formula.

## The building blocks

| Component | Role | Typical examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Binder** | Forms the film, binds pigments, provides adhesion and durability | Styrene acrylic, pure acrylic, vinyl acrylic emulsions |
| **Prime pigment** | Opacity (hiding) and whiteness | Titanium dioxide (TiO2) |
| **Colour pigments** | Colour | Organic and inorganic pigments, stainers |
| **Extenders / fillers** | Bulk, cost reduction, sheen control, film reinforcement | Calcium carbonate, talc, china clay, silica |
| **Dispersant / wetting agent** | Wets and stabilises pigment particles | Polyacrylate dispersants, surfactants |
| **Thickener / rheology modifier** | Viscosity, sag resistance, brushing, levelling | Cellulosic, HASE, HEUR thickeners |
| **Defoamer** | Controls foam in manufacture and application | Mineral oil, silicone defoamers |
| **Coalescent** | Helps film formation of harder binders | Ester alcohols, glycol ethers |
| **Biocides** | In-can preservation and in-film protection | In-can preservatives, algaecides, fungicides |
| **pH adjuster** | Stability | Ammonia, amines |
| **Water** | Carrier | — |

## How the paint is made

1. **Grind stage (mill base):** water, dispersant, wetting agent, defoamer and part of the thickener are mixed; TiO2 and extenders are added and dispersed at high speed until the target fineness is reached.
2. **Let-down stage:** speed is reduced and the **binder emulsion**, coalescent, remaining thickener, biocides and water are added.
3. **Adjustment:** viscosity, pH and colour are corrected; the batch is filtered and filled.

Binders are shear-sensitive — add them in let-down, not the high-speed grind.

## PVC: the master variable

**Pigment Volume Concentration (PVC)** is the volume of pigment + extender as a percentage of the total dry film volume.

- **Low PVC** (well below CPVC): binder-rich, glossier, more washable and durable — premium exteriors and enamels.
- **Medium PVC**: balanced sheen and cost — most interior emulsions.
- **High PVC** (near or above CPVC): matt, porous, lower cost — distempers and economy emulsions.

Above **CPVC**, there isn't enough binder to wet all particles; air voids appear, and scrub and stain resistance fall off sharply. Binder quality and particle size strongly influence how far you can push PVC. See the [glossary](/learning-center/glossary) for definitions.

## Where cost hides

- **TiO2** is usually the most expensive ingredient. Better dispersion and spacing (and functional extenders) can maintain hiding at lower TiO2.
- **Binder** is often the second-largest cost. A binder with **higher binding power** can hold more extender at the same scrub resistance — reducing cost per litre even if its price per kg is higher.
- **Additive efficiency**: the right dispersant reduces grind time and improves colour strength.

## Performance tests to run

- Viscosity and stability (including heat-age storage)
- Opacity/contrast ratio
- Scrub / wash resistance
- Sheen/gloss
- Water and alkali resistance
- Early rain resistance (exteriors)
- Dirt pickup and weathering (exteriors)

## Key takeaways

- A paint = **binder + pigments + extenders + additives + water**.
- **PVC vs CPVC** controls performance and cost more than any single raw material.
- The **binder** decides durability; a stronger binder can lower total formula cost.
- Optimise with **ladder studies**, not guesswork.
