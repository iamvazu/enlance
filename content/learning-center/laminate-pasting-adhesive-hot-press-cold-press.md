Decorative laminate — universally called **sunmica** in India — is on almost every modular kitchen, wardrobe and office desk. Pasting it onto plywood, MDF or particle board looks simple, yet **laminate bubbling, edge lift and peeling** are among the most common callbacks for furniture makers. This guide explains how to choose and use a **laminate pasting adhesive** on both **cold press** and **hot press** lines.

## The three ways laminates are pasted

1. **Hand pasting with rolling/weights** — common in site carpentry. Fast and cheap, but pressure is uneven. Suitable only for small panels.
2. **Cold press** — a hydraulic or screw press at room temperature. Panels stay under pressure for a longer period while the PVAc adhesive sets.
3. **Hot press** — a heated hydraulic press that accelerates curing so cycles are much shorter. Used by factories and high-volume modular units.

## Choosing a laminate adhesive

A good **synthetic resin laminate adhesive** needs:

- **Smooth, even spreadability** by roller or glue-spreader machine.
- **Adequate open time** so large sheets can be laid up before pressing.
- **Good wet tack** so laminates don't slide in the press.
- **Fast set under pressure** (especially for hot press cycles).
- The right **durability class**: **D2** for interior furniture, **D3** for kitchens, bathrooms and humid regions. See [D1–D4 classes explained](/learning-center/d1-d2-d3-d4-wood-adhesive-classes-en-204-explained).

## Cold press process

1. **Condition** boards and laminates together for at least a day.
2. **Clean** the board surface and laminate back of dust.
3. **Spread** adhesive uniformly on the board with a notched spreader or roller.
4. **Lay up** the laminate within open time, aligning edges.
5. **Stack** panels in the press with flat cauls/platens; keep stacks square.
6. **Press** evenly for the time recommended for your adhesive and room temperature — longer in cool, humid weather.
7. **Rest** panels flat after pressing and allow full cure before trimming and edge-banding.

## Hot press process

1. Same preparation and spreading as cold press.
2. **Load quickly** — heat starts the cure immediately, so closed assembly time matters.
3. **Set platen temperature and cycle time** within the adhesive's recommended range. Too hot can cause blistering or discolouration of thin materials; too short leaves an under-cured glue line.
4. **Unload and stack flat** to cool. Don't trim hot panels immediately.

Always run a trial when you change adhesive, laminate brand or board supplier.

## Hot press vs cold press at a glance

| Factor | Cold press | Hot press |
|---|---|---|
| Capital cost | Lower | Higher |
| Cycle time | Longer | Much shorter |
| Throughput | Small–medium units | Factories, high volume |
| Sensitivity to closed assembly time | Lower | Higher |
| Energy use | Minimal | Heating required |
| Adhesive requirement | Good open time, wet tack | Fast set under heat, heat stability |

## Why laminates bubble — and how to fix it

- **Dry patches** from uneven spreading → use a notched spreader or machine; check spread visually on each sheet.
- **Trapped air** → lay laminate from one edge or the centre outward; use proper press pressure.
- **Low or uneven pressure** → flat platens/cauls, correct stack height, even clamping.
- **Moisture mismatch** between board and laminate → condition materials together.
- **Early trimming** → allow full cure before routing and edge-banding.
- **Wrong grade in wet areas** → upgrade to D3.

## Edge lifting after installation

Edge lift months after installation is usually moisture attacking the glue line through unsealed edges. Always **edge-band or seal every edge**, use **D3 adhesive near sinks and hobs**, and raise base units off wet floors. ## Key takeaways

- Use a **press** for anything larger than a small panel.
- **Even spread + correct pressure + full cure** prevents almost all bubbling.
- **D2** for interiors, **D3** for wet zones.
- Trial every new combination of adhesive, board and laminate.
