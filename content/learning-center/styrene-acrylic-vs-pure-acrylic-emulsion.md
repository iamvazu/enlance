Every emulsion paint starts with a binder choice, and for most formulators in India the shortlist is two families: **styrene acrylic emulsion** and **pure acrylic emulsion**. Both are water-based acrylic copolymers made by emulsion polymerisation. The difference is in the monomers — and that difference shows up in cost, UV resistance and how the paint ages.

## What's the difference?

- **Styrene acrylic emulsion** is a copolymer of **styrene** with acrylic monomers (such as butyl acrylate). Styrene is hard, hydrophobic and relatively inexpensive.
- **Pure acrylic emulsion** is made from **acrylic and methacrylic monomers only** (for example methyl methacrylate with butyl or 2-ethylhexyl acrylate).

Styrene's aromatic ring absorbs UV light, which over time can cause **chalking and yellowing** outdoors. Pure acrylics don't have that weak point, so they hold gloss and colour longer in sunlight.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Property | Styrene acrylic | Pure acrylic |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Lower | Higher |
| UV resistance / exterior durability | Good (economy–mid exteriors) | Excellent (premium exteriors) |
| Gloss & colour retention outdoors | Moderate | High |
| Water resistance | Very good (hydrophobic styrene) | Very good |
| Alkali resistance (fresh plaster) | Very good | Very good |
| Pigment binding at high PVC | Very good | Good |
| Yellowing tendency | Some, in strong UV | Minimal |
| Typical uses | Interior emulsions, primers, distemper, putty, texture, economy exteriors | Premium exterior emulsions, sheen finishes, elastomeric coatings |

## When to choose styrene acrylic

Choose a **styrene acrylic emulsion** when cost efficiency and binding power matter most:

- **Interior emulsions** at economy and mid price points
- **Wall primers and sealers**, where alkali and water resistance are key
- **Acrylic distempers** and **wall putty**
- **Texture paints**, which need strong binding of coarse aggregates
- **Economy exterior paints** at higher PVC

## When to choose pure acrylic

Choose a **pure acrylic emulsion** when the paint must look good outdoors for years:

- **Premium exterior emulsions** with long durability claims
- **Sheen and gloss finishes** where retention matters
- **Light and bright colours** that must not yellow
- **Elastomeric and crack-bridging coatings**, where flexibility is needed

## Blending strategies

Many formulators **blend** styrene acrylic and pure acrylic binders to balance cost and performance — for example, a pure acrylic–rich binder in a premium exterior and a styrene acrylic-rich binder in its economy sibling. Ladder studies (varying the ratio and measuring scrub, gloss retention and weathering) are the reliable way to find the sweet spot.

## Other binder properties to check

- **MFFT and Tg** — decide film formation and hardness. See [MFFT and Tg explained](/learning-center/mfft-and-tg-emulsion-polymers-explained).
- **Particle size** — finer particles improve penetration and binding.
- **Solids content** — affects cost per unit of polymer and formulation latitude.
- **Surfactant system** — influences water sensitivity, foam and stability.

## Key takeaways

- **Styrene acrylic** = cost-effective, strong binding, great for interiors, primers, putty and textures.
- **Pure acrylic** = premium UV durability, gloss and colour retention for exteriors.
- **Blends** and ladder studies help hit a price-performance target.
