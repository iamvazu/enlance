For textile printers in Tiruppur, Erode, Ludhiana or Kerala, winning export orders increasingly depends on **chemical compliance**. Global brands publish **restricted substance lists (RSLs)** and **manufacturing restricted substance lists (MRSLs)** that limit certain chemicals in both the finished garment and the chemicals used to make it. Two groups come up constantly for pigment printing: **APEOs** and **formaldehyde**.

## What are APEOs?

**Alkylphenol ethoxylates (APEOs)** — including nonylphenol ethoxylates (NPEOs) — are surfactants that were widely used in emulsion polymerisation, detergents and textile auxiliaries. They break down into **alkylphenols** that are persistent and toxic to aquatic life. Many regulators and brand programmes now restrict them.

In binders, APEOs could enter through the **surfactants used to make the emulsion** or through **auxiliaries** added to the print paste.

## Why formaldehyde matters

Some crosslinking chemistries used in textile finishing and older binder systems can **release formaldehyde**. Formaldehyde is a known irritant and is regulated in textiles in many markets, with tighter limits for babywear and skin-contact items.

Modern **self-crosslinking acrylic binders** can deliver wash fastness with low or no formaldehyde release, depending on chemistry.

## What this means for your print room

1. **Ask for documentation.** For every binder, thickener, fixer and softener, request a statement on APEO content and formaldehyde, and any third-party certifications your buyers recognise.
2. **Audit your whole recipe.** A compliant binder won't help if the fixer or softener isn't.
3. **Balance fastness.** Moving away from formaldehyde-based fixers may require a better self-crosslinking binder or adjusted curing to hold fastness.
4. **Keep records.** Maintain a chemical inventory with supplier declarations — auditors will ask.
5. **Test finished goods.** Periodically test prints for restricted substances through an accredited lab.

## Compliance checklist

- [ ] Binder declared APEO-free
- [ ] Binder and fixer: formaldehyde status documented
- [ ] Thickener, softener, emulsifier: declarations collected
- [ ] SDS on file for all chemicals
- [ ] Recipe fastness re-validated after any substitution
- [ ] Periodic lab testing of finished goods

## Key takeaways

- Export buyers restrict **APEOs** and **formaldehyde** across textile chemicals.
- Compliance is about the **whole recipe**, not just the binder.
- **Self-crosslinking acrylic binders** help maintain fastness without formaldehyde-based fixers.
- Collect **supplier declarations** and keep records.
