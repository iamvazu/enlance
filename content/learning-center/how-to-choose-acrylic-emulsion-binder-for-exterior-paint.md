Exterior walls in India face some of the harshest conditions any paint film sees: intense UV, 40 °C+ surface temperatures, driving monsoon rain, high humidity, alkaline new plaster and airborne dirt. The **emulsion binder** is what decides whether an exterior emulsion still looks good after three monsoons. This guide lists the properties that matter most and how to choose.

## 1. UV durability

UV breaks polymer chains at the film surface, causing **chalking, fading and gloss loss**. **Pure acrylic** and high-acrylic binders offer the best UV resistance; **styrene acrylics** are cost-effective for economy exteriors. See [styrene acrylic vs pure acrylic](/learning-center/styrene-acrylic-vs-pure-acrylic-emulsion).

## 2. Early and long-term water resistance

- **Early rain resistance** — can a freshly painted wall survive a shower a few hours after application? Depends on binder, surfactants and coalescence.
- **Long-term water resistance** — resistance to blistering and softening during weeks of monsoon.
- **Water vapour permeability** — the film should let moisture in the wall escape, or it may blister.

Low-surfactant, hydrophobic binders and correct coalescence improve all three.

## 3. Alkali resistance

Fresh cement plaster is highly alkaline. Binders must resist **saponification** and efflorescence. Acrylic and styrene acrylic binders both perform well, but a good **alkali-resistant primer** remains essential on new walls.

## 4. Dirt pickup resistance (DPUR)

Soft, low-Tg films stay slightly tacky in the heat and hold dust — grey, streaky walls in a year. Harder binders or **self-crosslinking** grades reduce dirt pickup. The balance is flexibility: too hard and the film cracks with the substrate. See [MFFT and Tg explained](/learning-center/mfft-and-tg-emulsion-polymers-explained).

## 5. Flexibility and crack bridging

Thermal cycling opens hairline cracks in plaster. **Elastomeric** exterior coatings use low-Tg pure acrylic binders at high binder levels to bridge cracks — a premium segment with strong waterproofing claims.

## 6. PVC and CPVC

**Pigment Volume Concentration (PVC)** is the proportion of pigment and extender in the dry film. Exterior paints are typically formulated **below CPVC**, so the binder fully surrounds every particle — giving a closed film with good water and dirt resistance. Economy exteriors push closer to CPVC for cost; binder quality becomes critical there.

## 7. Algae and fungal resistance

Humid, shaded walls in Kerala and the coast grow algae and fungus. Binder alone doesn't solve this; **in-film biocides/algaecides** and hydrophobic films together do. Choose binders compatible with your biocide package.

## Binder selection matrix

| Exterior segment | Binder direction | Key tests |
|---|---|---|
| Economy exterior | Styrene acrylic, higher PVC | Water resistance, alkali, cost |
| Mid-range exterior | Styrene acrylic / acrylic blend | Scrub, early rain, DPUR |
| Premium exterior | Pure acrylic, lower PVC | QUV/weathering, gloss & colour retention, DPUR |
| Elastomeric / waterproof | Low-Tg pure acrylic, high binder | Elongation, crack bridging, water uptake |

## How to run a binder evaluation

1. Fix a **control formula** with your current binder.
2. Substitute the candidate binder on an **equal solids** basis.
3. Adjust coalescent for film formation.
4. Test: viscosity stability, scrub, early rain, water uptake, alkali resistance, DPUR, and **exterior exposure panels** or accelerated weathering.
5. Compare **cost per litre** of finished paint, not binder price per kg.

## Key takeaways

- Prioritise **UV durability, water resistance, alkali resistance and dirt pickup** for Indian exteriors.
- **Pure acrylic** for premium, **styrene acrylic** for economy, **blends** in between.
- Formulate **below CPVC** for closed, durable films.
- Test on **real exposure** — Kerala's monsoon is the ultimate lab.
