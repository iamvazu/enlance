Switching or adding a polymer emulsion supplier can lower costs, reduce lead times and de-risk your supply chain — but only if the new binder performs exactly as expected, batch after batch. This checklist sets out a structured way for paint, construction chemical, textile and coir manufacturers to **qualify a new emulsion supplier**.

## Stage 1: Desk review

Request and review:

- **Technical Data Sheet (TDS)** — solids, viscosity, pH, MFFT, Tg, particle size, ionic nature, recommended uses.
- **Safety Data Sheet (SDS/MSDS)** — hazards, handling, transport.
- **Certificate of Analysis (CoA)** format — which parameters are tested per batch?
- **Quality system** — ISO certification, QC lab capability, retain-sample policy.
- **Compliance documents** where relevant — for example restricted-substance statements for textile exports.
- **Manufacturing** — do they polymerise in-house or re-sell/trade?

## Stage 2: Lab evaluation

1. **Verify TDS values** on the sample: solids, viscosity, pH, MFFT.
2. **Direct substitution** in your control formula on an equal-solids basis.
3. **Performance tests** relevant to your product:
   - Paints: scrub, opacity, gloss, water/alkali resistance, early rain, dirt pickup, heat-age stability.
   - Construction: bond strength, water absorption, flexibility, open time with cement.
   - Textiles: rub and wash fastness, hand, runnability.
   - Coir: bond strength, flexibility, ageing.
4. **Compatibility**: with your thickeners, dispersants, biocides, pigments and cement.
5. **Stability**: heat-age and, if relevant, freeze-thaw.

## Stage 3: Plant trial

- Run a **full-size batch** on your normal equipment.
- Watch for **foaming, shear stability, grit/fineness, filterability** and viscosity drift.
- Compare **cost per litre / per kg of finished product**, not binder price per kg.
- Keep **retain samples** of trial batches for later comparison.

## Stage 4: Consistency check

One good sample doesn't prove a supplier. Ask for **three consecutive production batches** and check:

- CoA values vs your own measurements
- Variation in viscosity, solids, pH and MFFT
- Any difference in your finished product

## Stage 5: Commercial and logistics

- **Lead time** to your plant and **freight cost** per tonne
- **Minimum order quantity** and packaging (drums, IBCs, tankers)
- **Payment terms** and price-revision mechanism (monomer-linked?)
- **Technical support** — who do you call when a batch misbehaves?
- **Custom grade development** capability

## Key takeaways

- Qualify in **stages**: documents → lab → plant → consistency → commercials.
- Measure **cost in use**, not price per kg.
- Insist on **three-batch consistency** before switching fully.
- Value **technical support and proximity** — they save money when things go wrong.
