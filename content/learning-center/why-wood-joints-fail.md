When a glued joint fails, the first question is always *"Was it the glue?"* Sometimes it is. More often it's how the glue was chosen or used. The good news: **the broken joint tells you what went wrong**. This guide walks through nine common causes of wood adhesive bond failure and how to prevent each one.

## First, read the break

Look closely at the failed surfaces:

- **Wood fibres torn on both faces (wood failure)** — the adhesive was stronger than the wood. The joint failed from overload or wood defects, not the glue.
- **Clean glue film on one face, bare wood on the other (adhesive failure)** — the glue didn't wet or penetrate one surface. Think contamination, skinned glue or a dense/oily surface.
- **Glue film split through the middle (cohesive failure)** — the adhesive itself was weak: wrong grade, moisture softening, under-cure or diluted glue.
- **Very little glue visible anywhere** — a **starved joint**.

## 1. Wrong durability class for the environment

**Symptom:** joints and laminates fine at handover, failing after months in kitchens, bathrooms or monsoon.
**Fix:** use a **D3** adhesive in any wet or humid zone. See [EN 204 classes explained](/learning-center/d1-d2-d3-d4-wood-adhesive-classes-en-204-explained).

## 2. Starved joint

**Symptom:** dry-looking glue line, little squeeze-out, clean break.
**Causes:** too little adhesive, over-clamping thin glue out, very absorbent end grain.
**Fix:** spread a uniform film; pre-size end grain; aim for a small, even bead of squeeze-out.

## 3. Glue skinned before closing

**Symptom:** adhesive failure on one face, often a glossy, un-wetted film.
**Causes:** exceeding open time on hot, dry days or under fans.
**Fix:** work in smaller sections, close joints faster, avoid direct airflow on spread glue.

## 4. Contaminated or glazed surfaces

**Symptom:** glue peels cleanly off one surface.
**Causes:** dust, oil, wax, silicone, old polish, or a burnished surface from dull cutters.
**Fix:** machine fresh surfaces, clean thoroughly, lightly sand glazed faces.

## 5. Wrong wood moisture content

**Symptom:** slow cure, weak joints, later cracking or gaps.
**Causes:** gluing wet timber or boards stored outdoors in the monsoon; or very dry wood absorbing water too fast.
**Fix:** condition materials indoors; target roughly **8–12%** moisture.

## 6. Insufficient or uneven pressure

**Symptom:** gaps, bubbles under laminate, weak spots.
**Fix:** more clamps, cauls to spread load, proper cold/hot press for panels. See the [laminate pasting guide](/learning-center/laminate-pasting-adhesive-hot-press-cold-press).

## 7. Machined or loaded too early

**Symptom:** joints creep or open slightly; edges lift after trimming.
**Fix:** allow full cure before routing, sanding, edge-banding or loading.

## 8. Diluted or poorly stored adhesive

**Symptom:** thin, watery glue; weak films; lumps or skins in the drum.
**Causes:** adding water, freezing, heat exposure, containers left open.
**Fix:** don't dilute unless the TDS allows it; store in a cool, shaded place with lids closed. See [how to store wood adhesive](/learning-center/how-to-store-wood-adhesive-shelf-life).

## 9. Joint design and wood movement

**Symptom:** cracks along or beside the glue line across seasons.
**Causes:** cross-grain gluing of wide solid wood, no allowance for movement, poor joint fit.
**Fix:** design for wood movement; rely on good-fitting joinery, not glue alone, to bridge gaps. PVAc adhesives are not gap fillers.

## A quick diagnostic flow

1. Is the failure in a **wet or humid** area? → check durability class first.
2. Is there **clean adhesive film** on one face? → surface prep or open time.
3. Is the **glue line split** through the middle? → grade, dilution, cure.
4. Is there **almost no glue**? → spread rate and pressure.
5. Is **wood torn** on both faces? → the glue did its job; look at loading or timber.

## Key takeaways

- The **break pattern** tells you the cause.
- Most failures are **process** problems: spread, open time, pressure, moisture, cure.
- In wet zones, the **durability class** is non-negotiable.
