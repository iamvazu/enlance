Pigment printing is the most widely used textile printing method in India — simple, versatile and water-efficient. Unlike dyes, pigments have no affinity for fibres. What holds the colour on the fabric is the **binder**: a water-based polymer that forms a thin, flexible film locking pigment particles to the fibre surface. Get the binder right and prints survive rubbing and repeated washing while staying soft.

## The pigment print paste

A typical print paste contains:

- **Binder** — acrylic copolymer emulsion that forms the film
- **Pigment dispersion** — the colour
- **Thickener** — synthetic thickener for print definition
- **Fixer / crosslinker** (optional) — improves fastness
- **Softener** — improves hand
- **Emulsifier, anti-foam, urea or humectant** — runnability and screen protection
- **Ammonia or pH adjuster**

## How the binder works

After printing, the fabric is **dried and cured** (typically in a stenter or curing chamber). During curing, binder particles coalesce into a film and — in **self-crosslinking** binders — form chemical crosslinks that make the film tougher and more wash resistant.

## The fastness–hand balance

| Want more… | Usually means… | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Rub fastness** | Harder film, more binder, crosslinking | Stiffer hand |
| **Wash fastness** | Crosslinking, full cure | Needs proper curing temperature |
| **Soft hand** | Softer (lower Tg) binder, lower add-on | Can reduce dry/wet rub fastness |
| **Brightness** | Fine, clear film | Binder clarity matters |

Choosing a binder with the right **Tg and crosslinking** for your fabric and market is the key. See [MFFT and Tg explained](/learning-center/mfft-and-tg-emulsion-polymers-explained).

## Common problems and fixes

- **Poor dry rub fastness** → increase binder-to-pigment ratio, check curing, avoid over-pigmentation.
- **Poor wet rub fastness** → use self-crosslinking binder or fixer; ensure full cure.
- **Poor wash fastness** → under-curing is the usual culprit; verify stenter temperature and dwell time.
- **Stiff hand** → softer binder, lower add-on, add softener, reduce fixer.
- **Screen choking / roller build-up** → binder with good re-emulsifiability and mechanical stability; add humectant; don't let paste dry on screens.
- **Print bleeding** → thickener balance and viscosity.

## Compliance matters

Export buyers increasingly require **APEO-free, formaldehyde-free** (or low-formaldehyde) chemistry and compliance with restricted-substance lists. See [APEO-free and formaldehyde-free textile binders](/learning-center/apeo-free-formaldehyde-free-textile-binders).

## Key takeaways

- The **binder is the glue** of pigment printing.
- Balance **rub and wash fastness** against **soft hand** through binder choice and recipe.
- **Curing** is non-negotiable for fastness.
- Choose binders with good **runnability** to avoid screen choking.
