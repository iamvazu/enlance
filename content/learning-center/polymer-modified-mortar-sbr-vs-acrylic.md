Kerala's monsoon, coastal humidity and flat-roofed homes make **waterproofing** one of the most important building products in South India. At the heart of most cementitious waterproofing coatings, repair mortars and bonding agents is a **polymer emulsion** — usually **SBR latex** or an **acrylic polymer**. This article explains how polymer modification works and how to choose between the two.

## How polymers improve cement mortar

Ordinary cement mortar is strong in compression but brittle, porous and weak in adhesion to old concrete. Adding a polymer emulsion changes that:

- As cement hydrates and water leaves, polymer particles **coalesce into films** threaded through the cement matrix.
- These films **bridge micro-cracks**, **block capillary pores** and **glue** cement particles and aggregates together.

The result:

- **Better adhesion** to old concrete, tiles and substrates
- **Lower water permeability and absorption**
- **Higher flexural and tensile strength**
- **Improved flexibility** and crack resistance
- **Better workability and water retention** (less plastic shrinkage)
- **Improved abrasion and chemical resistance**

## SBR latex vs acrylic polymer

| Property | SBR latex | Acrylic polymer |
|---|---|---|
| Chemistry | Styrene-butadiene rubber copolymer | Acrylic / styrene acrylic copolymer |
| UV & ageing resistance | Lower (butadiene can oxidise and embrittle) | **Higher** — preferred for exposed surfaces |
| Colour | Can darken/yellow over time | Stays stable |
| Water resistance | Very good | Very good |
| Flexibility | Good | Good to excellent (tunable Tg) |
| Cost | Generally lower | Generally higher |
| Typical uses | Bonding coats, repair mortars, concealed waterproofing | Exposed terrace/roof coatings, elastomeric membranes, decorative & exposed mortars |

Many formulators use **SBR for concealed and repair applications** and **acrylic polymer for exposed, UV-facing coatings**.

## Typical applications

- **Terrace and roof waterproofing** — two-component cementitious or elastomeric acrylic coatings.
- **Bathroom and wet-area waterproofing** — brush-applied cementitious slurries under tiles.
- **Basement and water tanks** — polymer-modified cementitious coatings (check potable-water suitability for tanks).
- **Repair mortars** — structural and non-structural patch repairs.
- **Bonding agents** — slurry coats to bond new mortar to old concrete.
- **Plaster modification** — improving crack resistance and water repellency in exterior plasters.

## Formulation pointers

- **Polymer–cement ratio** (by solids) drives performance; higher ratios increase flexibility and waterproofing but add cost.
- Use **defoamers** — polymer emulsions entrain air in mortar, which lowers strength.
- Check **compatibility with cement** — some emulsions coagulate with high-calcium systems; construction grades are designed to be cement-stable.
- Ensure **proper curing** — polymer-modified mortars generally need moist curing initially, then air curing to let the polymer film form.

## Key takeaways

- Polymer modification improves **adhesion, waterproofing, flexibility and durability** of cement systems.
- **SBR**: cost-effective for concealed and repair uses.
- **Acrylic**: better UV and ageing resistance for exposed waterproofing.
- Test **polymer–cement ratio**, air content and curing for your product.
