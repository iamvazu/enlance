Tile adhesives, grouts and wall putty are high-volume construction products where a small amount of polymer makes a big difference. This guide explains the role of **acrylic and styrene acrylic polymer emulsions** in these products and what formulators should test.

## Liquid polymers vs redispersible powders

- **Redispersible polymer powders (RDP)** are used in one-component dry-mix products — bag-and-add-water tile adhesives and putties.
- **Liquid polymer emulsions** are used in **two-component** systems (powder + liquid), **ready-mix pastes** (such as acrylic wall putty and ready-to-use tile adhesives), and as site-added **admixtures** to improve ordinary cement mixes.

Enlace's polymer emulsions serve the liquid side of this market.

## Tile adhesives and grouts

A polymer improves:

- **Adhesion** to low-porosity tiles (vitrified, porcelain) that plain cement struggles to bond.
- **Flexibility** (deformability) — important for large-format tiles, external walls and heated floors.
- **Open time and adjustability** — more time to place and align tiles.
- **Water resistance** in wet areas.

For grouts, polymers add **stain resistance, flexibility and reduced cracking**.

## Wall putty and skim coats

Wall putty levels plaster before painting. Polymers provide:

- **Adhesion** to plaster and concrete, reducing flaking.
- **Cohesive strength and hardness** to resist scratching and sanding damage.
- **Water resistance** — essential in humid and coastal areas to prevent putty softening and paint peeling.
- **Good sandability** at the right polymer level.

**Acrylic wall putty** (ready-mix paste) is formulated with a **styrene acrylic or acrylic binder**, fillers and additives — essentially a very high-PVC coating. See [emulsion paint formulation basics](/learning-center/emulsion-paint-formulation-basics).

## What to test

| Product | Key tests |
|---|---|
| Tile adhesive | Tensile adhesion (dry, water-immersed, heat-aged), open time, slip, deformability |
| Grout | Flexural/compressive strength, water absorption, shrinkage, stain resistance |
| Wall putty | Adhesion, hardness, water resistance, sandability, shelf stability (paste) |

## Formulation tips

- Match the **polymer's Tg** to the flexibility needed; softer polymers give more deformability.
- Control **air entrainment** with defoamers.
- Check **cement compatibility** for cementitious systems and **in-can stability** for paste products.
- Use **in-can preservatives** for ready-mix pastes to prevent spoilage.

## Key takeaways

- Polymers upgrade tile adhesives, grouts and putty with **adhesion, flexibility and water resistance**.
- **Liquid emulsions** suit two-component systems, pastes and site admixtures.
- Test adhesion **after water immersion** — it's where cheap formulations fail.
