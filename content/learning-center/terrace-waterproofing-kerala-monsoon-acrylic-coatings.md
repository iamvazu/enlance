Kerala receives some of India's heaviest monsoon rainfall, and flat concrete terraces take the brunt of it. Hot sun followed by sudden rain, standing water on poorly sloped roofs, and hairline slab cracks create the perfect conditions for **leaks, damp ceilings and spalling**. Modern **acrylic polymer waterproof coatings** are one of the most effective answers.

## Why terraces leak

- **Thermal cycling:** slabs expand in the sun and contract in rain, opening and closing cracks.
- **Ponding:** poor slope or blocked outlets leave water standing for days.
- **Porous concrete and plaster:** capillaries let water migrate through.
- **Weak details:** parapet junctions, pipe penetrations and expansion joints.
- **Ageing of old treatments:** brick-bat coba and rigid coatings crack over time.

## Two main coating families

**1. Polymer-modified cementitious coatings**
A cement-based powder mixed with a liquid **acrylic or SBR polymer**. The polymer adds adhesion, flexibility and low permeability. Excellent under tiles and in wet areas; on exposed terraces, choose flexible grades and acrylic rather than SBR for UV durability. See [SBR vs acrylic](/learning-center/polymer-modified-mortar-sbr-vs-acrylic).

**2. Elastomeric acrylic coatings**
Paint-like, highly flexible coatings based on **low-Tg pure acrylic emulsions** at high binder levels. They stretch across moving cracks (crack bridging), reflect heat in white shades and resist UV. Often applied in multiple coats, sometimes with fibre mesh at cracks and joints.

## What the polymer must deliver

| Property | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Elongation & crack bridging | Accommodates slab movement |
| UV resistance | Exposed to direct sun year-round |
| Low water uptake | Prevents softening and whitening under ponding |
| Adhesion to concrete | Prevents peeling |
| Dirt pickup resistance | Keeps reflective white roofs cool and clean |
| Alkali resistance | Direct contact with cementitious substrates |

These properties come from polymer design — monomer choice, Tg, particle size and crosslinking. See [MFFT and Tg explained](/learning-center/mfft-and-tg-emulsion-polymers-explained).

## Application best practice (for contractors)

1. **Repair the surface** — remove loose material, fill cracks with a flexible filler, fix slopes and outlets.
2. **Clean and prime** as the system requires.
3. **Reinforce details** — mesh at cracks, parapet corners and pipes.
4. **Apply the specified number of coats** at the specified thickness — thin coats fail early.
5. **Allow proper cure** before rain; check the weather window.
6. **Maintain** — keep outlets clear and inspect annually before the monsoon.
