Decorative veneer brings the look of solid teak, walnut or oak to plywood and MDF panels at a fraction of the cost — but thin veneers are unforgiving. Too much adhesive bleeds through and ruins the finish; too little leaves blisters and loose patches. This guide covers how to **glue veneer** reliably with a synthetic resin wood adhesive.

## Understand the veneer

- **Thickness:** decorative veneers are often well under a millimetre. The thinner the veneer, the greater the bleed-through risk.
- **Porosity:** open-grained species (oak, ash, some teak) let adhesive pass through more easily.
- **Moisture:** veneers exposed to humid air can curl and expand; condition them flat, under light weight, in the workshop.

## Choose the right adhesive

- Use a **D2** adhesive such as [Enfixx HD](/products/enfixx/hd) for interior furniture; **D3** ([Enfixx MARINO](/products/enfixx/marino)) for kitchens and humid regions.
- Viscosity should be high enough not to soak straight through, yet spread thinly and evenly.

## Spread control is everything

- Spread on the **substrate**, not the veneer.
- Apply a **thin, uniform film** with a fine-notched spreader, roller or glue-spreader machine.
- Avoid puddles and thick edges — they're where bleed-through starts.
- Use the [coverage calculator](/tools/coverage-calculator) to estimate quantity, then fine-tune on scrap.

## Pressing

- Lay the veneer within **open time**.
- Use a **cold press, hot press or vacuum bag** for even pressure across the whole surface. Hand-rolling alone isn't sufficient for panels.
- Place **release paper or a protective sheet** between veneer and platen to catch any bleed-through.
- For hot presses, keep temperature and time within the adhesive's recommended range — excessive heat can drive adhesive into the veneer.

## Common problems

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Bleed-through (glue spots on face) | Too much adhesive, low viscosity, very thin/porous veneer, excess heat | Reduce spread, use higher-viscosity grade, protect press, lower heat |
| Blisters / bubbles | Dry spots, trapped air, uneven pressure | Uniform spread, proper press, lay from centre outward |
| Loose edges | Glue starved at edges, early trimming | Spread fully to edges, allow full cure |
| Cracks/checks in veneer | Moisture change, over-dry veneer | Condition veneer, finish promptly, seal |
| Glue lines visible at joints | Squeeze-out in veneer joints | Tape joints properly, clean squeeze-out |

## Finishing

- Let panels **cure fully** before sanding — sanding soft glue can pull fibres.
- Sand lightly; veneers are thin.
- Seal promptly with the chosen finish to stabilise moisture.

## Key takeaways

- **Thin, uniform spread on the substrate** is the key to avoiding bleed-through.
- Use a **press or vacuum** — not hand rolling — for panels.
- Condition veneers and **cure fully** before sanding.
